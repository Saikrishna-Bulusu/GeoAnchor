Classical baselines on real satellite reference -- AnyVisLoc env80
326 frames (Scene_09 n=134, Scene_10 n=192), cold start, satellite basemap.
Energy and latency are Pi 5 pinned, 1 tile / 2048 ref keypoints.

A fix is USABLE if it is accepted at a gate and lands inside 20 m. A
matcher that returns confident fixes on the wrong building is not cheap,
it is broken -- so joules per FIX is the wrong denominator.

ORB is 7.2x cheaper per fix than xfeat_mnn and returns ZERO usable fixes
in 326 frames -- 0/192 plausible on Scene_10, 5/134 on Scene_09, never
reaching a 5% accept rate at any gate. SIFT's one qualifying gate has a
MEDIAN error of 126.6 m. AKAZE manages 9 plausible solves out of 326.

So the 'ORB is 25x cheaper, maybe it is enough' hypothesis is dead. The
energy saving is real and it buys nothing, because the denominator is
zero. Report this as a negative result -- it is the cleanest argument in
the project for why a learned detector is necessary rather than merely
nicer, and it costs no extra experiment to make.
